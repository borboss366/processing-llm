# Creature polish pass — captures (Brief 5)

Module: `web/app/loaded-modules/creature.js`, captured over the techno mix
@120 s **with Butterchurn ON** (primary, per acceptance) via
`tools/capture-creature.mjs`; black-background stills kept as structural
diagnostics.

## Acceptance statements (factual)

- **Is the whole body visibly moving on the beat?** Yes. Quadruped: root
  bounces at 2× beat, body leans into the stride, head bobs at 2× with the
  0.25 lag; legs step in diagonal pairs with visible knee bend. Jelly: bell
  scale-pulses ±20% and drifts upward on contraction; tentacles sway
  together.
- **Are feet solid?** Yes. 9-node pinned paws + floor clamp with contact
  friction: feet plant as units on the ground line; the crumpled puddles
  from the first pass are gone (compare `creature-quadruped.png`).
- **Are tentacles continuous?** Yes. Ring-chain sampling (12 rings, 2–3
  nodes each, rail+rung+diagonal springs) replaced k-nearest for limbs: legs
  are continuous ladders, tentacles hang as unbroken ribbons; no bead
  clusters.

## What changed vs Brief 4 captures

Scale/framing (65% canvas height, feet on a `ground` line), additive
rendering (part-hue faces α0.32, SDF-classified boundary outline with a
wide low-alpha glow pass, dim interior edges, nodes only near joints, and a
soft per-node "flesh" pass that merges into a glowing body mass), root
motion (bounce/lean/swim), knees + 9-node paws + floor, ring-chain limbs.

Two deviations worth knowing:
- `blendMode(ADD)` is additive *within* the p5 canvas (scoped push/pop);
  cross-canvas compositing over Butterchurn remains normal alpha — true
  additive would need CSS `mix-blend-mode` on the fg canvas, a page-level
  change out of module scope.
- Boundary edges are classified by SDF surface proximity, not "edge in
  exactly one triangle": on a sparse k-NN graph the triangle rule marks half
  the interior as boundary (first re-capture rendered as bright confetti).

## Remaining nits (visible in captures)

The head outline is partly broken (sparse sampling on a small circle); the
two near-side legs overlap the far pair in side view, so feet can visually
cross. Both are shape-tuning, not structure.
